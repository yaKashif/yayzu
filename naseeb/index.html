Naseeb: The Jeep Driver

You are Naseeb, a jeep driver in the far north, where the roads are cut into cliff faces and a landslide can close the way overnight. Today's job: take the jeep up the mountain.

About the game

Naseeb makes a living taking people and supplies where no bus will go, in an old army-green jeep: a 3.9-litre straight six, three gears in high and low range, part-time four-wheel drive, open differentials, leaf springs and drum brakes. It's a free driving game you play in your browser, and the jeep is built up from its tires with real physics.

Nothing is faked. Each tire touches the ground wherever it comes nearest, so it climbs the edge of a log or a stone, and grips along a slope, not across it. Grip comes from how fast the tread slides over the ground. Weight shifts as you climb, brake and lean, the leaf springs let each axle twist under the body, and the chassis scrapes on what it can't clear. Lift a tire out of a deep rut and an open differential sends it all the spin.

You hear it too: the six firing cylinder by cylinder, gravel cracking under the tread, tires squealing when they slide, and a thump when a wheel lands hard.

How to play

  • Drive: hold the throttle and the automatic picks the gear.
  • Low range for the hard bits: press 1 for Low 1st, the most pull and the slowest speed. 1 to 6 pick the gears, Low and High; 0 hands back to the automatic.
  • Brake and reverse: hold ▼ to brake; held once you've stopped, it backs up. ▲ then stops it and drives off again in the gear you had.
  • Tight hairpins: some are too tight to get round in one go. Stop, back up, and go again.
  • Off the road, or rolled? A Back on the road button puts you back where you left it (or press Backspace).
  • The engineer's view: press F to see the forces at every tire, the slip, the loads and the springs.

The mountain

A narrow dirt track climbs 150 m up the forested face of a mountain in six legs, about 1.8 km of it, the mountain on one side and the drop on the other. Some hairpins need a three-point turn; others go round in one go if you take the right line. On the way: ruts worn deep by other jeeps, potholes, water bars, fallen logs and branches, stones in the road, and stretches where it squeezes down to a jeep's width. A clock times each climb, and remembers your best.

Snow peaks stand over the valley, clouds drift past and their shadows cross the slopes, and dust hangs behind you on the dry track. Drive it in the morning, the afternoon or the evening light.

Tips

  • Take the hairpins slowly in Low 1st, swing wide, and back up before the front wheels reach the edge.
  • Crawl the logs in Low 1st, over the stones other drivers left as steps. Hit them fast and the jeep bucks.
  • Stuck with a wheel spinning in a deep rut? Lock the differentials with L.
  • On a slower computer, set Graphics to Standard in the settings.

Questions

Is Naseeb free?

Yes. It's free to play, with no account and nothing to download.

Does it work on my phone?

Yes. On-screen pedals, steering and gear buttons work on phones and tablets, upright or sideways.

How real is the jeep?

Its engine, gearing, wheelbase, track, weight, tires and brakes follow the published figures for a short-wheelbase 1970s jeep, 3.84 m long. A few things aren't published and are estimated: the steering lock, the air drag, the springs and dampers, and how the weight splits between the engine, body and other parts.

Why does one tire spin while the others don't move?

That's an open differential. It gives both tires on an axle the same torque, so a tire in the air or on slick ground, which can't push back, gets all the spin. Locked differentials make both tires turn together.
