Naseeb

Drive a 1970 Toyota Land Cruiser FJ40, built up from its tires with real physics. Every tire grips, slips and spins on its own, from dry asphalt to sand, mud, snow and ice.

About the game

Naseeb is a free driving physics sandbox you play in your browser. The truck is modelled on the published specs of the 1970 FJ40: the 3.9-litre F six with its torque curve, the 3-speed gearbox and two-speed transfer case, 4.11 axles, part-time four-wheel drive with open differentials, drum brakes and H78-15 tires, at its 1,480 kg kerb weight.

Nothing is faked. Each tire's grip comes from how fast its tread is sliding over the ground, so it grips, peaks and lets go like a real tire. Weight shifts onto the rear tires when you accelerate, onto the front when you brake, and onto the outside in a turn. The differentials work the way real ones do, so with one side on ice, that side's tires spin while the other side's barely turn.

Naseeb is being built a piece at a time. Right now it's the FJ40's rolling chassis: the ladder frame, engine, gearbox, transfer case, axles and wheels. The body and leaf spring suspension come next.

How to play

  • Drive: hold the throttle and the automatic picks the gear.
  • Gears: press 1 to 6 to choose one yourself. The six are the FJ40's own: its three gears in low and high range. Press 0 to hand back to the automatic, R for reverse.
  • Brake with Space or the brake pedal.
  • Try every surface: the chips at the bottom put the truck on each one.
  • See the forces: arrows show the weight, the ground pushing back at each tire, and the friction it gets from the ground.

Surfaces

The test track runs through dry asphalt, concrete, wet asphalt, gravel, grass, sand, mud, packed snow and ice, each with its own grip and its own give. Soft ground lets the tires sink in and drags at them. The last strip puts the left tires on ice and the right ones on asphalt, a classic test of a differential.

Tips

  • Stuck on the ice and asphalt strip? Lock the differentials in the settings and try again.
  • Pull away in Low 1st on sand or mud: more torque, less spin.
  • Turn on slow motion to watch a tire break loose and grip again.
  • Let some air out of the tires in the settings and see how the contact patch grows.

Questions

Is Naseeb free?

Yes. It's free to play, with no account and nothing to download.

Does it work on my phone?

Yes. On-screen pedals, steering and gear buttons work on phones and tablets, upright or sideways.

How close is it to a real FJ40?

The engine, gearing, wheelbase, track, weight, tires and brakes follow published figures for the 1970 FJ40. A few things aren't published and are estimated: the steering lock, the air drag, and how the weight splits between the engine, body and other parts.

Why does one tire spin while the other doesn't move?

That's an open differential, the kind the FJ40 came with. It gives both tires on an axle the same torque, so a tire on ice, which can't push back, gets all the spin. Locked differentials make both tires turn together.
